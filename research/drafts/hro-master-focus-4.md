## Canonical hierarchy

1. **Set, execute, and improve performance**
   - **Goals and expectations** → Employee: request/create or revise a goal; acknowledge goals; align a personal goal to team priorities. Manager: approve, return, cascade, or realign a goal; resolve an objective conflict. HR: publish a goal-cycle/template; monitor goal completion/quality. Administrator: configure goal fields, cycle dates, eligibility, and integrations.
   - **Check-ins and feedback** → Employee: request a check-in; submit self-reflection; request/give feedback; acknowledge feedback. Manager: schedule/complete check-in; record feedback; request multi-rater input; create improvement support plan. HR: launch/monitor feedback campaign; investigate overdue or unfair process patterns. Administrator: configure questionnaires, anonymity, retention, and notifications.
   - **Performance review and calibration** → Employee: submit review; view/acknowledge outcome; appeal/correct factual record. Manager: complete review; propose rating; submit evidence; participate in calibration; communicate outcome. HR: open/close review; facilitate calibration; audit distribution/equity; resolve exceptions/appeals. Administrator: configure rating scales, routing, visibility, and audit access.
   - **Performance improvement** → Employee: acknowledge improvement plan; submit progress evidence; request support. Manager: initiate/update/close improvement plan; document milestones and outcomes. HR: review high-risk plan; approve extension/closure where policy requires; report case trends. Administrator: apply restricted access, legal hold, and retention policy.

2. **Learn, build skills, and progress a career**
   - **Skills and capability profile** → Employee: add, validate, or retire a skill; request skill assessment; publish internal opportunity preferences. Manager: validate proficiency; identify team skill gap; nominate employee for assessment. HR: maintain skills framework; approve equivalencies; analyze capability gaps. Administrator: manage taxonomy, proficiency scales, assessment-provider connection, and privacy rules.
   - **Learning and certification** → Employee: discover/enrol/withdraw from learning; request learning approval/funding; submit completion; record certification; request renewal. Manager: approve/decline learning request; assign learning; verify work application. HR: curate learning pathway; administer budget and mandatory learning; verify certification evidence/expiry. Administrator: integrate LMS/provider; configure catalog, compliance rules, credit, and certificates.
   - **Career development and mentoring** → Employee: create development plan; request career conversation; find/request/change mentor; record mentoring goal. Manager: co-author development plan; approve stretch assignment; sponsor mentoring match. HR: run mentoring program; match/monitor participants; publish career architecture/pathways. Administrator: configure matching, eligibility, safeguarding/confidentiality, and reporting.

3. **Deploy and retain talent**
   - **Internal mobility** → Employee: express interest/apply for internal opportunity; update mobility preferences; request transfer/secondment; withdraw application. Manager: approve release/transfer timing; propose project, rotation, or secondment; provide internal reference. HR: publish opportunity; manage mobility case; adjudicate policy exception; measure movement/fairness. Administrator: configure internal-only visibility, eligibility, recruiting/HRIS integrations, and conflict controls.
   - **Talent review and succession** → Manager: nominate talent; provide performance/potential/readiness evidence; identify critical role risk; propose successor/development action. Employee: provide career aspiration/readiness input where policy permits; acknowledge development action. HR: convene talent review; validate criteria; maintain critical-role and successor slate; approve succession plan; monitor coverage/diversity. Administrator: configure confidential talent grids, access segmentation, cycle, and retention.
   - **Recognition and retention** → Employee: send recognition; redeem/acknowledge recognition; request correction. Manager: grant award/recognition; approve monetary recognition. HR: run recognition program; audit equitable access and tax/payroll handling; analyze retention signals. Administrator: configure budgets, award catalog, approval limits, and payroll integration.

4. **Listen, plan workforce, and support organizational change**
   - **Engagement and surveys** → Employee: respond to survey; opt out where allowed; submit confidential comment; view approved results/action updates. Manager: view authorized team results; create/complete action plan; request comment follow-up. HR: design/launch/close survey; enforce anonymity threshold; analyze results; publish action plan; manage vendor. Administrator: configure audience, anonymity, translations, access, retention, and integrations.
   - **Workforce and headcount planning** → Manager: submit hiring/headcount demand; propose role/skill/location change; provide scenario assumption; confirm plan execution. HR: model demand/supply/gaps; produce workforce scenario; reconcile plan; approve workforce action plan; monitor plan versus actual. Administrator: maintain position/org/job data, planning model, data feeds, and role-based planning access.
   - **Organizational change support** → Employee: receive/acknowledge change communication; ask question; complete assigned transition learning; request support. Manager: assess local impact; submit change plan; complete stakeholder/people actions; communicate approved change. HR: assess people impacts/risks; design consultation and transition support; approve workforce-change package; track adoption. Administrator: manage change-case workspace, restricted documents, audit trail, and retention/legal-hold rules.

## Boundary and alias notes

- **Performance goals** are commitments and assessment evidence; **development goals** belong in career/L&D but can be linked, not duplicated. “OKRs,” objectives, priorities, targets, and goals are aliases with local methodology overlays.
- **Feedback** is not an engagement survey; survey comments are aggregate listening data. “Pulse,” climate, eNPS, and engagement survey are survey variants, not interchangeable measures.
- **Skills** are capabilities/assertions; **certifications** are externally or internally evidenced credentials with issuer, validity, expiry, and verification state. Do not equate course completion with proficiency.
- **Talent review** identifies/assesses talent; **succession** creates coverage/readiness for critical roles; “9-box,” potential review, and talent calibration are confidential review methods, not parent journeys.
- **Internal mobility** includes transfers, rotations, project gigs, secondments and redeployment. It intersects recruiting/ATS and employee changes, while final employment/position transactions remain core HR/recruiting-owned.
- **Headcount planning** is a numerical/budgeted subset of workforce planning; workforce planning additionally covers skills, supply, demand, scenarios, and deployment. Position control and requisition approval may be finance/recruiting overlays.
- Organizational change support includes people-impact, communication, consultation and adoption actions; it excludes legal determination, payroll execution, and transaction execution, though agents must route to those owners.
- Local overlays must govern ratings, appeal rights, works councils/unions, survey anonymity, recognition tax treatment, credential requirements, language/accessibility, data residency, retention, and who may see potential/succession information.

## Key facts

- CIPD characterizes performance management as a two-way process of regular, open, supportive discussion; it includes objectives, feedback, reviews, ratings, and L&D. Its factsheet favors regular/timely improvement-focused feedback and notes movement away from forced ranking toward quality conversations. https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/performance-factsheet
- CIPD says talent management spans attraction, identification, development, engagement, retention and deployment; succession planning prepares high-potential employees for critical/future roles, while deployment includes rotations, learning, project work and secondments. https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/talent-factsheet
- CIPD’s workforce-planning stages support separate routable outcomes for operating-environment understanding, workforce analysis, future needs/scenarios, gaps, action planning, and monitoring/evaluation; implementation requires stakeholder consensus, responsibilities, manager support and data continuity. https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/workforce-planning-factsheet
- OPM’s employee-engagement material supports a manager/agency distinction: managers make development opportunities visible and approve attendance, while agencies operate broader engagement strategy. https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/training-and-development/reference-materials/online-courses/maximizing-employee-engagement/content/common/cw/data/OPM_MEE_EngagementStrategyTechniques_FINAL_20130927.pdf
- CIPD’s change-management factsheet identifies change management as a distinct people-management concern and highlights consequences of ineffective change management. https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/change-management-factsheet

## Source quality assessment

- **Tier 1:** CIPD factsheets (professional body; evidence-informed definitions/process scope) and US OPM guidance (government employer guidance). These are appropriate for canonical boundaries, though not jurisdiction-specific legal advice.
- **Tier 2:** SHRM talent-management topic material is an established professional-industry source, useful for corroboration but less specific than the CIPD factsheets. https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/topics/talent-management
- **Tier 3:** None relied upon for taxonomy claims.

## Gaps and unanswered questions

- Confirm target countries, works-council/union coverage, public-sector rules, and regulated roles before defining final approval and retention overlays.
- Define canonical data model: job architecture, position management, skill ontology/proficiency source, critical-role criteria, potential/readiness definitions, and evidence standard.
- Decide whether potential ratings, calibration notes, survey free text, mentoring notes, and PIP records are separately access-segmented and whether employees can access/correct each.
- Set ownership boundaries with ATS, LMS, payroll/recognition, finance planning, ER/legal, document management, and analytics platforms.
- Specify human-in-the-loop policy for recommendations, bias testing, explanation, override/audit, and prohibitions on automated adverse employment decisions.
