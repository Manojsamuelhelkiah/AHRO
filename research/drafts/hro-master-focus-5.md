## Canonical hierarchy

1. **Resolve employee concern or conduct matter**
   - **Employee relations and grievance resolution**
     - Record an informal workplace concern and request facilitated resolution.
     - Lodge a formal grievance with the required statement and supporting evidence.
     - Acknowledge a grievance and issue the applicable procedure and timeline.
     - Conduct a fair grievance investigation and preserve case evidence.
     - Schedule a confidential grievance meeting with companion/representation options.
     - Issue a grievance finding, remedy, and implementation actions.
     - Process a grievance appeal and communicate the final outcome.
     - Refer a mutually agreed matter to mediation.
   - **Workplace investigation**
     - Triage a misconduct, harassment, discrimination, retaliation, ethics, or whistleblowing allegation.
     - Open a restricted investigation and assign an independent investigator.
     - Apply interim risk controls, including separation, leave, no-contact, or safeguarding measures.
     - Collect witness, document, system, and physical evidence with chain-of-custody records.
     - Conduct and document a witness or subject interview.
     - Produce a substantiated, unsubstantiated, or inconclusive investigation finding.
     - Close an investigation with need-to-know notifications, remedial actions, and retention controls.
   - **Disciplinary action**
     - Request conduct/performance disciplinary review.
     - Issue a written notice of allegations and disciplinary hearing invitation.
     - Arrange representation, interpreter, accessibility, and hearing accommodations.
     - Record disciplinary hearing evidence and employee response.
     - Issue a proportionate disciplinary outcome and corrective-action plan.
     - Process an employee disciplinary appeal.
     - Monitor completion of a warning, training, coaching, or conduct condition.
   - **Ethics and speak-up**
     - Submit a confidential ethics or conflict-of-interest disclosure.
     - Assess a retaliation risk and activate protected-reporting controls.
     - Resolve a conflict-of-interest disclosure with mitigation or recusal actions.
     - Close an ethics case with auditable outcome evidence.

2. **Protect health, safety, wellbeing, and work ability**
   - **Occupational health and safety**
     - Report a workplace hazard, near miss, injury, illness, or safety incident.
     - Escalate an imminent-danger or serious-harm safety event.
     - Initiate an incident investigation and corrective-action record.
     - Request occupational-health assessment or fitness-for-work review.
     - Record work restrictions and communicate only functional limitations.
     - Verify completion of safety corrective actions and required reporting.
   - **Accommodation and work adjustment**
     - Request a disability, medical, religious, pregnancy, or other workplace accommodation.
     - Obtain consent and supporting information for an accommodation assessment.
     - Evaluate essential functions and identify reasonable accommodation options.
     - Approve, decline, or offer an alternative accommodation with rationale.
     - Implement an accommodation plan and restricted-access review schedule.
     - Review, amend, or close an accommodation when circumstances change.
   - **Wellbeing and EAP**
     - Request confidential wellbeing resources or an EAP referral.
     - Make a manager-supported, consent-based EAP referral.
     - Escalate an immediate welfare or safeguarding concern to emergency protocol.
     - Document non-clinical workplace support without storing clinical detail.

3. **Manage workforce representation, policy, and compliance**
   - **Labor, union, and works council**
     - Identify applicable collective agreement, union, works-council, or consultation obligation.
     - Notify and consult employee representatives on a proposed covered change.
     - Process a collective grievance, arbitration, or labor-dispute referral.
     - Maintain bargaining, consultation, and agreement-obligation records.
   - **HR policy, audit, and compliance**
     - Request interpretation of an HR policy or local legal overlay.
     - Identify a compliance exception and assign remediation.
     - Produce an HR case, record, access, or policy-control audit evidence package.
     - Execute a records-retention hold, deletion, or lawful access request.
     - Report suspected policy breach, fraud, or record tampering.

4. **Enable mobility, location changes, and compliant remote work**
   - **Immigration and global mobility**
     - Assess right-to-work, visa, work-permit, sponsorship, or immigration-document requirement.
     - Initiate a new-hire, extension, amendment, or termination immigration case.
     - Request cross-border work authorization before travel or work begins.
     - Record immigration document verification and expiry follow-up.
     - Escalate a work-authorization expiry or non-compliance risk.
   - **Relocation and domestic transfer**
     - Request domestic transfer, location change, or transfer-letter processing.
     - Request a relocation assessment, package, vendor coordination, or reimbursement.
     - Approve transfer/relocation funding and effective-date change.
     - Complete a transfer with payroll, tax, benefits, identity, and reporting-line updates.
   - **Remote and cross-border work**
     - Request a remote, hybrid, temporary overseas-work, or cross-border-work arrangement.
     - Assess employment-law, tax, social-security, data-security, immigration, and permanent-establishment risk.
     - Approve, deny, or time-limit a remote-work arrangement.
     - Update work location, schedule, equipment, safety, and manager obligations.
     - Review or revoke a remote-work arrangement upon changed risk or business need.

5. **Execute organizational change and employment exit**
   - **Restructure and redundancy**
     - Request restructuring workforce-impact assessment.
     - Identify roles in scope and produce a protected selection-audit record.
     - Initiate representative consultation, individual consultation, or statutory notification workflow.
     - Issue an at-risk notification and consultation meeting invitation.
     - Evaluate redeployment, alternative employment, or voluntary-exit options.
     - Approve redundancy selection, severance, notice, and local statutory calculations.
     - Issue redundancy outcome and complete exit actions.
   - **Termination and resignation**
     - Submit voluntary resignation and requested last-working-day details.
     - Validate resignation notice, acceptance, notice waiver, and handover plan.
     - Request involuntary termination review and legal/HR approval.
     - Issue termination decision, notice, final-pay instructions, and required documentation.
     - Process settlement/release, separation agreement, or contested-exit review.
     - Stop access and complete final pay, benefits, equipment, and records actions.
   - **Offboarding, retirement, death in service, alumni, references, and rehire**
     - Create an offboarding plan with owner, dates, asset return, access removal, and knowledge transfer.
     - Confirm retirement eligibility and process retirement election/transition.
     - Activate death-in-service protocol, verified next-of-kin communications, and benefits escalation.
     - Issue an employment verification or approved reference.
     - Register alumni opt-in and maintain consented alumni relationship data.
     - Assess rehire eligibility, prior-record restrictions, and required approval.
     - Complete rehire record linkage without inappropriate access to restricted prior-case data.

## Boundary and alias notes

- **Complaint vs grievance vs investigation:** a grievance is an employee employment concern; an investigation is the evidence-gathering method. A grievance may require an investigation; not every investigation is a grievance.
- **Performance management is excluded** unless it becomes a formal conduct/disciplinary, accommodation, or termination outcome.
- **Accommodation vs leave/benefits:** accommodation owns workplace adjustments and functional restrictions; leave and benefits administration own entitlement/payment, with a controlled handoff.
- **EAP is not clinical care:** route resource/referral and consent only; crisis, medical, and safeguarding pathways require human emergency protocols.
- **Safety aliases:** “incident,” “accident,” “near miss,” “hazard,” “unsafe condition,” and “injury” require different severity and regulatory-routing rules.
- **Mobility overlaps payroll/tax/security:** HR operations should orchestrate; tax, immigration, security, and payroll remain accountable for their domain decisions.
- **Remote work is not automatically mobility:** domestic remote work may be a workplace arrangement; overseas or cross-border work invokes separate tax, social-security, data, immigration, and employment-law assessment.
- **Redundancy aliases:** reduction in force/RIF, layoff, retrenchment, severance, collective dismissal, and redundancy need jurisdiction-specific mapping; do not treat them as equivalent globally.
- **Termination vs resignation vs retirement:** all are exits, but authority, notices, benefits, final pay, and dispute risks differ.
- **Death in service:** restrict data and communications to verified authority, designated internal teams, and lawful next-of-kin processes.
- **Rehire:** “boomerang,” reinstatement, and reemployment are related but distinct; reinstatement after dispute may preserve service differently from a new rehire.

## Key facts

- UK guidance says employers should maintain written disciplinary rules/procedures, inform staff, and align them to the Acas Code; failure to follow the Code can increase tribunal awards by up to 25% in relevant cases. This supports explicit procedural, notice, and appeal leaves rather than a generic “discipline” intent.  
  Source: https://www.gov.uk/taking-disciplinary-action
- Acas describes a formal grievance flow that includes accessible written procedure, investigation, a meeting with companion rights, a decision, and appeal; it also emphasizes fair, consistent, confidential handling. This supports distinct intake, investigation, meeting, decision, and appeal outcomes.  
  Source: https://www.acas.org.uk/grievance-procedure-step-by-step
- Under the U.S. OSH Act general-duty provision, employers must provide a workplace free from recognized serious hazards and comply with applicable OSHA standards. Safety intake therefore needs severity triage and corrective-action evidence rather than a simple HR ticket.  
  Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/oshact/section5-duties
- Employment exits encompass resignation, retirement, dismissal, and redundancy, each carrying different employer/employee rights and responsibilities; the taxonomy should preserve those separate routable outcomes.  
  Source: https://www.lra.org.uk/ending-employment
- U.S. employers use Form I-9 verification to establish identity and employment authorization for hires; right-to-work verification and document-expiry workflows should be distinct from general employee-data changes.  
  Source: https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central

- Define decision authority and delegation thresholds by worker population, geography, level, and legal entity.
- Confirm systems of record and the permitted medical/ethics/investigation data boundaries.
- Establish case confidentiality roles, legal-hold triggers, privilege handling, and audit-log retention.
- Define severity thresholds and emergency ownership for violence, self-harm, serious injury, and regulatory-reportable incidents.
- Confirm whether the product supports unionized/works-council populations directly or only consultation evidence workflows.
- Establish rehire eligibility policy, cooling periods, treatment of prior disciplinary records, and reinstatement distinctions.
