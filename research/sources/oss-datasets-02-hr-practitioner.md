# HR Practitioner Seed (v2)

A curated instruction-tuning seed for HR and recruiting assistants, built for the
[Adaption AutoScientist Challenge](https://adaptionlabs.ai/blog/autoscientist-challenge)
(Part 2, HR track).

**3,059 training rows + 244 held-out evaluation rows.**

## What this is

| Group | Rows | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grounded recruiting tasks | ~2,692 | real job adverts as context, 8 task types, 2 markets |
| Generic HR policy questions | 512 | questions only |
| Authored generalist HR questions | 99 | original, 12 practice areas |

All completions are empty by design and are synthesised during adaptation. That is a
deliberate choice, not an omission: there is no open HR instruction corpus with
completions worth training on (see _Sources considered and rejected_), so the value here
is in the prompts and their grounding.

## Held-out split

`hr_holdout.jsonl` contains 244 rows stratified across all 21 task types, capped so no
more than 25% of any stratum moves into eval - the authored practice areas hold only
~10 questions each. Carved out **before** training; prompt overlap with the training
file is zero.

## Fields

- `prompt` \- the task or question
- `context` \- the job advert the task is grounded in, where applicable
- `completion` \- empty; synthesised during adaptation
- `source` / `license` \- provenance
- `task` \- task type

## The eight grounded task types

Job-description rewriting, requirement extraction, competency-based interview question
generation, screening rubric design, candidate rejection communication, hiring-manager
summarisation, inclusive-language review, and passive-candidate outreach. Each advert
is assigned one task, rotating across the set.

## How it was built

- **Two advert markets, not one.** v1 grounded every task in the `lang-uk` corpus, which
comes from a single Ukrainian job board and leans heavily technical. v2 adds adverts
from a second market with a wider role mix (hospitality, healthcare, trades, retail).
- **Answers dropped from the policy source.** Every answer in `hr-policy-dataset`
describes an invented company ("NovaTech Solutions", a 60-day notice period, 9:30-6:30
hours). The questions generalise; the answers do not. Training on them teaches a model
to state fabricated specifics as fact, so only the questions were kept.
- **Contact details scrubbed.** Adverts were stripped of email addresses, phone numbers,
URLs and messaging handles. Note that part of the corpus arrives markdown-escaped
(`hr@example\.org`), which defeats a naive email regex; escapes are normalised before
matching. Bare company mail domains (`@example.com`) are removed too. Corporate social
handles without a dot (`@ExampleCo`) are deliberately kept - they are public marketing,
and removing them mangles legitimate advert text.
- **Adverts bounded and deduplicated.** English-language adverts only, 600-4,000
characters, deduplicated by title, then near-duplicates removed by TF-IDF cosine
similarity at 0.93.

### Sources considered and rejected

- **`job-search-distill`'s `resume_corpus` and `job_evals` tables.** The 9,855 candidate
evaluations are high quality, but they are keyed to 2,483 scraped resumes and are
meaningless without them. Re-publishing real candidates' CVs is not an acceptable
trade for a larger dataset, so only the `jobs` table was used.
- **`Nembi/llm-job-interview-dataset`** is labelled as an interview dataset but is
written entirely in Korean; excluded rather than silently degrading an English model.
- **`Shlok307/Interview_questions`** is technical interview preparation (neural network
architectures), not HR practice.

## Intended use and limitations

Intended for fine-tuning HR and recruiting assistants. Output is not legal advice.
Employment law is jurisdiction-specific and none of this data is jurisdiction-tagged,
so any compliance-sensitive guidance needs review by a qualified professional in the
relevant jurisdiction.

The advert corpora are English-language postings from two job boards. Coverage is broader
than v1 but is still not a representative sample of global hiring, and both skew toward
markets where online job advertising is the norm.

The 512 policy questions describe concerns common to salaried office employment and will
under-represent shift, seasonal and frontline work.
