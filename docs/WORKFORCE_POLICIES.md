# Workforce and process policy operating rules

## Purpose and limits

Workforce classification describes a **job**, not the person doing it. White-collar,
frontline and mixed work are organizational descriptors, not legal categories or
proof of ability, health, identity, eligibility, or entitlement. Site exposure
and safety criticality are separate facts. Leave unknown facts unknown.

Starter templates are expert-authored operational guidance. They are not legal
certification. Have the organization's appropriate legal, employment, medical and
site-safety authorities review jurisdiction, contract and applicability before
using a requirement. Do not infer police, drug or medical testing from collar
type. India PF/UAN, ESIC, minimum-wage and overtime requirements need separately
reviewed applicability; they are not universal frontline defaults.

Accessibility, preferred language and assisted-intake needs must be explicitly
collected. Never infer them from role, pay basis, nationality or workforce label.

## Configuration review checklist

Use **Settings → Workforce & Process Policies** for Starter templates, Matching
rules, Process steps, Preview & publish, and Recommendations. Use the job-profile
catalog in Settings to save reusable context; select a profile during intake.
Cases exposes workforce, site-exposure and safety-critical filters. Batch intake
creates held individual cases with separate snapshots; release is explicit and
does not bypass offer acceptance.

1. Establish tenant-owned job and site context, including country/state,
   operating environment, hazards, required licenses, employment arrangement,
   pay basis and shift/hiring mode. Do not invent facts for older cases.
2. Review the organization baseline and a starter policy. Confirm each step's
   evidence, accountable owner, authorized completer and enforcement point.
3. Test representative office, site and mixed cases as well as missing-context
   cases. Review combined AND/OR conditions and overlapping priorities.
4. Inspect the effective policy, not only the selected profile name. Confirm
   inherited obligations and any missing safety-context review requirement.
5. Publish only after scope and conflicts are resolved. A published version is
   intended for future matching intake, not an automatic rewrite of live cases.

## Safety and evidence

Assign a specialist from current organization members, with the relevant
case/item/site scope. Medical and HSE roles need independent assignments. A
named operational task uses a member's authenticated identity, not a display
name; administrator status alone does not override a different named assignee.
Revoke or regrant the exact scope when responsibility changes.

Medical fitness clearance, site safety sign-off and safety competency are
different evidence requirements. For an approved hazardous-site policy, one
cannot substitute for another. A checklist score, phone-call note, reminder,
agent assertion or unavailable provider cannot establish any of these facts.

Use designated human authority when no integrated provider can establish an
outcome. A human task records work that remains to be done; it does not imply a
medical, police, payroll, access or training provider has performed that work.

Medical source documents and findings belong in an appropriately restricted
source system. Operational views should expose only the minimum clearance state.
Do not paste diagnosis, examination results or other unnecessary medical details
into policy names, public checklist notes, event messages or recommendation text.

## Changes to active cases

Treat a role, site, hazard or policy change as a potential change to evidence
applicability. Review the impact before applying it; use a reason and current
version precondition. A stale browser tab must not overwrite newer changes.

Do not carry a safety clearance to a new site or materially different job merely
because a checklist item has the same identifier. Applicable evidence and final
human authority must be reconsidered. Completed historical records must not be
rewritten, and already-dispatched external work must not be repeated to make a
new policy snapshot appear complete.

Runtime process changes are accepted only at safe pre-workstream boundaries
while affected tasks remain unclaimed. A change after task action or after the
initialization boundary is rejected rather than replaying effects or replacing
the old evidence. Existing legacy policy snapshots retain their prior semantics;
newly published context-aware versions use additive mandatory constraints.

## Learning governance

Initial template recommendations are expert-authored, not learned. A learned
recommendation must identify its organization-local cohort, policy version,
date window, eligible population, usable sample, missingness and dated source
evidence. Demo cases and simulated effects are not verified outcomes.

Sparse observations mean insufficient evidence. A broader cohort, when used,
must be explicitly identified rather than presented as confidence about the
original narrow cohort. Correlation does not establish that a policy change
caused an outcome.

The broader-cohort view is descriptive only and cannot be published as a learned
default. It stays within the organization and compatible historical policy.
Manager-led learning is the connected human-task channel; the candidate portal
is for intake evidence, not an LMS or training-completion integration.

The first learning release is limited to supported operational adjustments such
as timing and accountable ownership. A reviewer must approve adoption; learning
must not remove compliance or safety gates, make employment decisions, pool
organizations' data without approval or change live case obligations silently.

## Operational verification

Before rollout, confirm tenant isolation and server-authoritative permissions,
legacy-profile compatibility, migration safeguards, and version conflicts. Test
new intake and per-person batch resolution, unknown context, retired profiles,
independent evidence gates, changed-context re-review, and recovery dispatch.
Recovery and retries must honor the same prerequisites and durable effect
identity as the initial request.

Use isolated test records without real contact authorization. A provider stub
inside one test process does not stop background workers in the running API
from discovering and contacting a test record.
