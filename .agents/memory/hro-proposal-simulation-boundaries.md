---
name: HRO proposal and simulation boundaries
description: Why language-based authoring remains a reviewed proposal and operational what-if results are not forecasts.
---

Plain-language authoring maps into existing supported configuration; it does not authorize new executors, weaken baseline safeguards or publish without human review.

**Why:** The selected product direction combines easier automation authoring with controlled execution. Treating generated prose as executable authority would bypass the existing professional-review and case-adoption boundaries.

**How to apply:** Keep generation read-only, disclose unsupported requests, bind acknowledgement to the current proposal and hand off an unpublished draft to the established editor.

Operational simulation starts with transparent deterministic assumptions, not predictive claims.

**Why:** The user explicitly chose forecasts only when historical evidence supports them. An aggregate mean cannot prove causal stage delays or the distribution of individual SLA failures.

**How to apply:** Separate measured and explicitly assumed baselines, retain missing evidence, label critical-path shares as assumptions and never derive case-level SLA rates from a mean.
