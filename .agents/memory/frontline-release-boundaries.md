---
name: Frontline release boundaries
description: Physical release proof and current validity must both govern downstream completion.
---
Frontline completion requires both an accountable recorded floor release and a fresh evaluation of independent evidence; neither alone is sufficient.

**Why:** A live readiness calculation does not prove that a person was released, while a historical release does not prove that medical, safety, statutory, or provisioning evidence remains valid. Separate checks that ignore each other's state can leave a case complete but operationally blocked.

**How to apply:** Keep canonical completion and downstream dispatch tied to the same live evaluation and recorded release. Expiry and revocation reopen human review without claiming that an unconnected physical-access system was remotely revoked.

Evidence eligibility must recheck the same current category-specific permissions required at capture; general case-edit and approval rights are not sufficient.

**Why:** A recorder can retain general approval access after a medical, payroll, identity, or other sensitive field grant is withdrawn. Trusting only the general grants would keep that recorder's evidence operationally eligible after the narrower authorization ended.

**How to apply:** Use the evidence's server-assigned category to evaluate current scoped grants during release and heartbeat reconciliation. Preserve independent evidence and human-review requirements rather than substituting administrator status.

Operational evidence summaries must not acquire private source references merely to support history or revocation controls.

**Why:** Medical and statutory record references can disclose protected information even when the underlying document is not returned.

**How to apply:** Use authority-gated, typed history for selecting durable records, and reauthorize the record's specific evidence class when revoking it. Do not reconstruct a revocation from a browser-supplied copy of private evidence.
